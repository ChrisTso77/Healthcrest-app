# Healthcrest reference-source policy

Owner preference, 28 October 2026: future upgrades should include reputable international references, including Harvard where relevant, to provide a broader perspective than NHS-only material.

Select sources for the claim and audience, not merely the publisher's reputation. Prefer relevant public-health guidance, primary research or well-conducted reviews. Distinguish these from academic educational summaries and service information; do not label every source a clinical guideline or imply endorsement of Healthcrest.

For additions and updates:
- Read the source and record its checked date, purpose, supported claims, population and limits.
- Include suitable international sources when they add context. Retain local sources for UK services and jurisdiction-specific advice.
- Explain differences in age bands, definitions, frameworks or recommendations. Do not average conflicting recommendations into an invented target.
- Do not turn population findings into personal predictions or validate animation rules through a habit citation.
- Keep source URLs, claim references, visible context, structured citations and tests aligned. Recheck moved links.
- Do not invent authorship, credentials, clinical review or publication dates.

Current expansion checked 28 October 2026:
- WHO physical activity: movement across daily life and inclusion of different populations.
- Harvard Healthy Eating Plate: academic nutrition education, food quality and cultural application; distinguish it from the NHS Eatwell framework and government guidance.
- CDC About Sleep: retain its 18–60, 61–64 and 65+ age bands alongside the NHS general adult range.
- WHO stress Q&A: routines, connection, news exposure and support in a global context.
